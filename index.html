Warn people during the call. Never drop it.

ShieldCall is research on detecting two things that caller ID cannot: a scam script, and a voice that is cloned or synthetic. It listens with consent, scores the language and the audio separately, and warns the person to verify. If it fails, the call goes on.

The problem

The call is where the damage starts

Caller-ID checks tell you whether a number was attested. They say nothing about what is being said, or whether the voice is real. With cheap voice cloning, a scammer can sound like an official, a bank, or a grandchild. By the time the call ends, the money has often moved.

A trusted voice

An official, a bank, a relative. Increasingly cloned or synthetic.

A believable script

Authority, then urgency, then secrecy: “do not tell anyone”.

A payment demand

Gift cards, wire, crypto, or a “safe account”. Paid before anyone checks.

The gap

No tool is listening to the conversation itself, so nobody warns the person during the call.

The solution

Listen with consent. Score two ways. Warn, never block.

ShieldCall runs beside the call, not inside it. It reads the language for scam tactics and scores the audio for signs of synthetic speech, then fuses the two and suggests a calm next step for the person on the line.

Consent first

Analysis happens only on audio the user or organization is authorized to provide. Raw audio is not kept by default.

A prompt, not a verdict

A warning means “verify independently”. It is never an accusation, and it never blocks a call.

Honest about limits

It abstains when unsure or out of its tested range. First targets are VoIP and contact-center channels, not native cellular audio.

Done

1. Data foundation

A locked, reproducible benchmark so that every later number can be checked and nothing is tuned on the test set.

  • Locked subset of ASVspoof 2019 LA (ODC-By), 67 held-out test speakers, attacks A07 to A19 never used for training
  • 14 telephone conditions, including real G.711, AMR-NB and Opus coding and noise down to 0 dB
  • Data card, annotation protocol and a fixed random seed
  • Synthetic scam-dialogue set for the language stream (Apache-2.0)

Official ASVspoof 2019 LA evaluation protocol, random sample of 6,000 trials (95% interval 8.8 to 11.6). *Organizers’ published baselines on the full list. We are at baseline level, not better. The audio is anechoic studio speech, so this does not describe real phone calls. On a 6,240-clip sample of ASVspoof 5, which has realistic source audio and 11 codec conditions, the same recipe scores 22.63% (95% interval 21.6 to 23.8; 16.9% with no codec, up to 28.6% for the worst codec). That is not comparable to the organizers’ baselines, which were trained on ASVspoof 5 data.

  • Four recipes tried; one rejected after it failed on the locked test. Errors double on ASVspoof 5
  • Prototype app built; iPhone build in Apple beta review
  • Next: harder validation, training on ASVspoof 5 data, minDCF, multiple seeds

3. Integration

Move from scored clips to a live call on one supported channel, with an explicit failure mode when quality is poor.

  • Live speech recognition on one user-controlled VoIP or contact-center channel
  • Latency budget and abstention rules when transcripts or audio are poor
  • Training and evaluation on ASVspoof 5 data (a first cross-corpus run is done) and on consented lab recordings

Not started. It depends on a channel with lawful access to call audio. First targets are not native cellular calls.

Planned

4. Safety and governance

Controls that must exist before anyone outside the lab relies on a warning.

  • Consent screens and a privacy policy exist in the app; not yet reviewed by a privacy professional
  • Data minimization, retention and deletion rules, access logging
  • Incident response, model cards, documented false-alert burden
Planned

5. Consented pilot

With a willing partner: warning latency, user comprehension, false-alert burden and reliability, in a published report that states its limits.

  • A partner willing to run a lab or consented pilot (none yet)
  • Pre-registered measures and a public report
