Warn people during the call. Never drop it.

ShieldCall is research on detecting two things that caller ID cannot: a scam script, and a voice that is cloned or synthetic. It listens with consent, scores the language and the audio separately, and warns the person to verify. If it fails, the call goes on.

The problem

The call is where the damage starts

Caller-ID checks tell you whether a number was attested. They do not tell you what is being said or whether the voice is real. By the time the call ends, the money has often moved.

These are reported losses, not total cost, and they describe the problem, not what ShieldCall has achieved. ShieldCall has not prevented any of it.

How it works

Two streams, fused by disagreement

Language stream

Reads the transcript for social-engineering stages: impersonating an authority, urgency, secrecy, then a payment demand. A 22-category pattern taxonomy runs on the device.

Audio stream

Scores 8 kHz telephone-bandwidth audio for artifacts of synthetic or vocoded speech, after simulating telephone distortion such as band-limiting, µ-law and packet loss.

Fusion and action

The streams are fused by disagreement, not averaged. The detector recommends monitor, warn, challenge, escalate, abstain or adapt, and it abstains when unsure.

Fail-open

The detector runs beside the call, not in it. If it is down, uncertain or out of its tested range, the call continues. Try “Simulate detector offline” above.

Consent and privacy

Analysis happens only on audio the user or organization is authorized to provide. Raw audio is not kept by default. A warning is a prompt to verify independently, never an accusation or a block.

Where it can run

First targets are user-controlled VoIP and enterprise or contact-center channels. It does not claim access to native cellular-call audio.

The acoustic component is a proxy-audio research baseline. Reported results in the repository separate confirmatory numbers from sanity checks.

Plan

Five stages, each with a public deliverable

  1. Data foundationLocked dataset manifest, lawful provenance, annotation protocol, speaker and attack-family splits, a held-out test set. Done when another engineer can reproduce the run.
  2. Modeling baselinesAcoustic, language and fusion baselines reported by speaker, codec, noise, language and attack family: false-positive and false-negative rates, EER, calibration, latency, abstention.
  3. IntegrationLive speech recognition on one supported VoIP or contact-center channel, with an explicit failure mode when quality is poor.
  4. Safety and governanceConsent, minimization, encryption, retention and deletion, access logging, incident response, model cards.
  5. Consented pilotWith a willing partner: warning latency, user comprehension, false-alert burden, reliability. A published report that states its limits.
